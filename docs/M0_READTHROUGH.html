LEDGER, from zero

First you watch one short game happen, move by move. Then a legend. Then five real boards to read. Budget fifteen minutes.

1 · The game in a minute

  • It is a turn game. You and one partner alternate single moves — 24 turns total, 12 each. Everything costs your turn: doing a job, making an offer, even asking a question. (The board calls a turn a "tick".)
  • The goal: 8 jobs exist. A finished job pays both players, but different secret amounts — job 3 might be worth 30 to you and nothing to them. You know your numbers, never theirs.
  • The money: jobs are paid from a shared pot of 100. Together via contracts, or alone via capped "draws" nobody can veto. Pot money left at the end is destroyed.
  • The catch: deals bind the instant they are accepted, and breaking one is legal — cheap for the breaker, often devastating for the other side.
  • Your score: your secret values of all finished jobs, plus money received, minus money paid. Not fairness, not the team.

2 · Watch one game

Played by the real engine. Each step shows the line it added to the game record, then what it means.

You
t1 you QUERY "which jobs matter most to you?"

Turn 1 — you spend your whole turn just asking a question. Talk is not free here: every turn is one action, and asking is the action.

Partner
t2 them INFORM "job 2 is my priority."

Turn 2 — your partner answers. You still do not know their exact numbers (values are private); you know only what they choose to tell you.

You
t3 you PROPOSE C1: job2->them f18, job5->you f12

Turn 3 — you put a deal on the table: you will do job 5 (funded 12 from the shared pot), they will do job 2 (funded 18). Nothing is binding yet; it is just an offer with an id, C1.

Partner
t4 them ACCEPT C1 [C1 locked; cancel window t5-t6]

Turn 4 — they accept, and the deal BINDS IMMEDIATELY: the pot money is set aside and the jobs become obligations. For the next two turns either side can still cancel for a fee of 1 — a cooling-off window.

You
t5 you WAIT

Turn 5 — you pass. Passing is also an action; the cooling-off clock runs.

Partner
t6 them WAIT [cancel window closed]

Turn 6 — they pass too. The window closes: from now on the only way out of C1 is to break it, which costs real money.

You
t7 you EXECUTE job5 [done]

Turn 7 — you do your half: job 5 is completed. It pays BOTH of you, each your own private amount. Your money is spent, your work is sunk — you are now counting on them to do their half.

Partner
t8 them RENEGE C1 [C1 reneged; refund 13; paid you 3]

Turn 8 — they break the deal instead. This is legal. They pay a penalty of 6, half of it to you — but their job will now never be done, and whatever it was worth to you is gone. Betrayal is cheap for the breaker and expensive for the exposed. This asymmetry is the point of the game.

—

Both sides eventually play END (or turn 24 arrives). Everything left in the pot is destroyed, any still-unfinished promised job is fined 8 against whoever promised it, and the final scores are settled.

Final score — you 3, partner 6. A score is: your private value of every finished job, plus payments received, minus payments and penalties paid. Nothing else. (For scale: perfect teamwork on this scenario could have reached 204 between you.)

3 · How to read a board

tick 9/24Turn 9 of 24. 'Tick' just means turn. You and your partner alternate; each of you gets 12 turns in a game.
POTThe shared money. Jobs are paid from it. Whatever is left at turn 24 is destroyed — money hoarded is money lost.
your draws 24/25How much pot money you have taken unilaterally, against your lifetime cap of 25. Draws need no permission and cannot be blocked.
SLOTSHow many jobs each of you can still personally do (3 per game). Doing a job costs a slot forever.
ACCOUNTPersonal money from side-payments and penalties. Can go negative.
JOB tableOne row per job. YOUR-COST / THEIR-COST are public prices to do it. YOUR-VALUE is what it pays you when finished — you never see their values; that is the whole game. NEEDS n means job n must be finished first.
f12In contract lines: 'funded with 12 from the pot'.
CONTRACTSOFFERED = a proposal on the table, not binding. LOCKED = binding: money reserved, jobs owed. Breaking a locked contract costs the breaker 6; never finishing an owed job costs 8 at the end.
HISTORYEvery turn so far, one line each, oldest first. Square brackets are the referee speaking: [C1 locked], [done], [refund 22].

4 · Now read five real boards

What counts as failure: not getting a detail wrong. Failure is a board that does not let you tell who is committed, who is exposed, and who is ahead — one you would have to reverse-engineer. Write first, then reveal, then judge.
