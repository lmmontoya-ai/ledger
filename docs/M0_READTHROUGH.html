LEDGER, from zero

First you watch one short game happen, move by move. Then a legend. Then five real boards to read. Budget fifteen minutes.

1 · The game in a minute

  • It is a turn game. You and one partner alternate single moves — 24 turns total, 12 each. Everything costs your turn: doing a job, making an offer, even asking a question.
  • The goal: 8 jobs exist. A finished job pays both players, but different secret amounts — job 3 might be worth 30 to you and nothing to them. You know your numbers, never theirs.
  • Two kinds of money — do not mix them up. The pot (100) is the shared budget for doing jobs: doing a job costs pot money. Your winnings are separate and come from nowhere: every finished job pays each player their own secret value on top. The pot limits what you can do, not what you can win — in the game below, perfect teamwork was worth 204 between the players, double the pot.
  • Spending the pot, two ways. Together, through a deal you both sign. Or alone, through a draw: you take pot money without asking, to fund a job you will do yourself — your partner sees it instantly and cannot stop it, but each player may take at most 25 per game this way. Pot money left at the end is destroyed, so hoarding it helps nobody.
  • The catch: deals bind the instant they are accepted, and breaking one is legal — cheap for the breaker, often devastating for the other side.
  • Your score: your secret values of all finished jobs, plus money received, minus money paid. Not fairness, not the team.

2 · Watch one game

Played by the real engine. Each step shows the line it added to the game record, then what it means.

You
turn 1 you CHAT "which jobs matter most to you?"

Turn 1 — you spend your whole turn just talking (the CHAT action). Talk is not free here: every turn is one action, and talking is the action.

Partner
turn 2 them CHAT "job 2 is my priority."

Turn 2 — your partner answers. You still do not know their exact numbers (values are private); you know only what they choose to tell you.

You
turn 3 you PROPOSE deal 1: them do job 2 (18 from pot) · you do job 5 (12 from pot)

Turn 3 — you put a deal on the table: you will do job 5 (funded 12 from the shared pot), they will do job 2 (funded 18). Nothing is binding yet; it is just an offer the board will list as deal 1.

Partner
turn 4 them ACCEPT deal 1 [deal 1 binds; cancel window turns 5-6]

Turn 4 — they accept, and the deal BINDS IMMEDIATELY: the pot money is set aside and the jobs become obligations. For the next two turns either side can still cancel for a fee of 1 — a cooling-off window.

You
turn 5 you WAIT

Turn 5 — you pass. Passing is also an action; the cooling-off clock runs.

Partner
turn 6 them WAIT [cancel window closed]

Turn 6 — they pass too. The window closes: from now on the only way out of deal 1 is to break it, which costs real money.

You
turn 7 you EXECUTE job 5 [done]

Turn 7 — you do your half: job 5 is completed. It pays BOTH of you, each your own private amount. Your money is spent, your work is sunk — you are now counting on them to do their half.

Partner
turn 8 them RENEGE deal 1 [deal 1 broken; 13 back to pot; they pay you 3]

Turn 8 — they break the deal instead. This is legal. They pay a penalty of 6, half of it to you — but their job will now never be done, and whatever it was worth to you is gone. Betrayal is cheap for the breaker and expensive for the exposed. This asymmetry is the point of the game.

—

Both sides eventually play END (or turn 24 arrives). Everything left in the pot is destroyed, any still-unfinished promised job is fined 8 against whoever promised it, and the final scores are settled.

Final score — you 3, partner 6. A score is: your private value of every finished job, plus payments received, minus payments and penalties paid. Nothing else. (For scale: perfect teamwork on this scenario could have reached 204 between you.)

3 · How to read a board

turn 9 of 24You and your partner alternate single moves; each of you gets 12 turns in a game.
POTThe shared budget for DOING jobs — costs come out of it. Your winnings do not: finished jobs pay your secret values on top. Pot left at turn 24 is destroyed.
DRAWSPot money taken without asking, to fund your own jobs — visible instantly, unblockable, capped at 25 per player per game.
SLOTSHow many jobs each of you can still personally do (3 per game). Doing a job costs a slot forever.
ACCOUNTPersonal money from side-payments and penalties. Can go negative.
JOB tableOne row per job. YOUR-COST / THEIR-COST are public prices to do it. YOUR-VALUE is what it pays you when finished — you never see their values; that is the whole game. NEEDS n means job n must be finished first.
DEALSOFFERED = a proposal on the table, not binding. BINDING = money reserved, jobs owed. Breaking a binding deal costs the breaker 6; never finishing an owed job costs 8 at the end.
HISTORYEvery turn so far, one line each, oldest first. Square brackets are the referee speaking: [deal 1 binds], [done].
